When you unlock the full 1,200 MHz of hashtag#spectrum, you get the space for wider, cleaner channels (think 80 MHz) with far less contention, which translates to more consistent performance in the places that hurt the most: dense venues, campuses, hospitals, warehouses… and yes, 𝐭𝐨𝐲 𝐟𝐚𝐜𝐭𝐨𝐫𝐢𝐞𝐬 at peak season.

The real multiplier is Standard Power + Automated Frequency Coordination (AFC). AFC makes it possible to use standard power responsibly (protecting incumbents) while giving 6 GHz the coverage and capacity needed to deliver real, scalable performance across entire buildings, campuses, and outdoor spaces. That’s how 6 GHz becomes a mainstream workhorse, not just a nice-to-have.

Now the bigger issue: 𝐠𝐥𝐨𝐛𝐚𝐥 𝐬𝐩𝐞𝐜𝐭𝐫𝐮𝐦 𝐡𝐚𝐫𝐦𝐨𝐧𝐢𝐳𝐚𝐭𝐢𝐨𝐧. If 6 GHz ends up fragmented country-by-country, we all pay for it—higher device complexity, slower ecosystem rollout, and features that don’t scale globally. We need more than the US/Canada and a few early adopters to join the party. To get the full benefit of hashtag#WiFi 6E/7 and what’s coming next, the world needs to align on 6 GHz access (and AFC) so innovation can scale everywhere.

Forward this Christmas wish and tag your local spectrum regulatory agency!

Help! My Multi Agent System is live. And the real challenge starts now: evaluating agentic AI in production - where traditional QA ends and reality begins.

In demos, everything works. This has and still is contributing to the AI Hype. In production, complexity explodes: Multi Agent Systems plan, re-plan, reason, and act—sometimes in ways we never anticipated. Traditional observability tools may show zero errors and fast response times, but beneath the surface, silent failures can cripple outcomes. Or worse, agents may spiral into recursive loops, racking up massive API bills—like the $47,000 agent failure, where agents talked to each other for 11 days before anyone noticed, reported by Teja from GetOnStack. That can happen to anyone if you don´t put the right controls in place. And there are more patterns observed like 
 
State Synchronization Failures: Agents operate on outdated or conflicting information or lose context across handoffs. For instance, a payment agent marks an order as paid, but the inventory agent misses the update and refuses to allocate stock
Runaway Loops: Recursive agent interactions can spiral out of control, consuming resources and incurring unexpected costs. 
Semantic Failures: Agents may complete tasks syntactically but miss the actual goal. These silent output failures are invisible to standard monitoring tools.

Most teams still rely on metrics built for microservices, not autonomous “minds.” The result: we debug probabilistic systems with deterministic rulers. The industry hasn’t solved this yet from our point of view.

One of our new initiatives within the Office of the CTO is going to focus on this problem space: how do you connect automated pre-prod evaluation results and in-production evaluation of multi agent systems in a meaningful way in order to improve the entire system continously. For that purpose we have identified a few key building blocks, amongst them are semantic collectors that produce agent-aware telemetry that indexes conversations, prompts, and tool calls, the need to define cognitive metrics which are native to the AI era—responsiveness, step efficiency, cost per interaction as well as the effective use of AI judges, LLM-based evaluators that validate task completion and flag semantic failures, even when code executes perfectly.

If you’re deploying agentic AI, don’t settle for “it runs.” after your evaluation in QA was succesful and up to the KPIs that you have defined. Create proof that it works and move from black-box deployments to measuring intent, reasoning, and real task completion.

We will report on lessons learned along the way as we did while we developed Extreme AI in Platform ONE - take a look at my previous posts and blogs. 

Love to hear your thoughts about this topic.
