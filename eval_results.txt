Q02 [General]
QUESTION: What are sticky clients and why are they a problem in Wi-Fi?

PIPELINE: fast  |  ROUTE: issue_tracking  |  DOCS: 10  |  TIME: 9.57s
RECALL: 4/5   PRECISION: 4/5

ANSWER:
    Sticky clients are Wi-Fi devices that remain connected to a specific access point (AP)
    even when a better AP is available nearby. This behavior often occurs due to outdated
    or inefficient roaming algorithms in the client device, which fail to make optimal
    steering decisions.

    The problem with sticky clients is that they degrade network performance by staying
    connected to an AP with weaker signal strength, leading to slower speeds, higher
    latency, and increased interference. This issue is particularly common in environments
    with multiple APs, such as mesh networks or enterprise setups.

    Meeting minutes from a Wi-Fi Alliance discussion highlight challenges in steering
    sticky clients, especially older devices, and emphasize the need for improved
    confidence in AP candidate lists to facilitate better roaming decisions [5]. Solutions
    like Wi-Fi Certified EasyMesh™ aim to address these issues by standardizing AP
    interoperability and optimizing client steering [2].

ANSWER:
    Wi-Fi 7 (802.11be) and Wi-Fi 8 are differentiated by their generational advancements
    in speed, efficiency, and features. Wi-Fi 7 focuses on enabling multi-gigabit speeds,
    extremely low latency, and enhanced reliability through features like Multi-Link
    Operation (MLO), 320 MHz channels, and 4K-QAM modulation [3][4]. It is optimized for
    applications like XR, automotive connectivity, and high-density environments.

    Wi-Fi 8, while not yet fully detailed or standardized, is expected to build on Wi-Fi 7
    by further enhancing spectral efficiency, power management, and support for emerging
    technologies such as AI-driven network optimization and advanced IoT interoperability.
    The focus will likely include more robust handling of ultra-low latency applications
    and improved performance in crowded RF environments. Specific technical details are

Q09 [2. TOPIC DEEP-DIVES]
QUESTION: What are sticky clients and why are they a problem in Wi-Fi?

PIPELINE: fast  |  ROUTE: issue_tracking  |  DOCS: 10  |  TIME: 0.02s
RECALL: 4/5   PRECISION: 4/5

ANSWER:
    Sticky clients are Wi-Fi devices that remain connected to a specific access point (AP)
    even when a better AP is available nearby. This behavior often occurs due to outdated
    or inefficient roaming algorithms in the client device, which fail to make optimal
    steering decisions.

    The problem with sticky clients is that they degrade network performance by staying
    connected to an AP with weaker signal strength, leading to slower speeds, higher
    latency, and increased interference. This issue is particularly common in environments
    with multiple APs, such as mesh networks or enterprise setups.

    Meeting minutes from a Wi-Fi Alliance discussion highlight challenges in steering
    sticky clients, especially older devices, and emphasize the need for improved
    confidence in AP candidate lists to facilitate better roaming decisions [5]. Solutions
    like Wi-Fi Certified EasyMesh™ aim to address these issues by standardizing AP
    interoperability and optimizing client steering [2].

PIPELINE: fast  |  ROUTE: topic_comparison  |  DOCS: 10  |  TIME: 11.87s
RECALL: 4/5   PRECISION: 4/5

ANSWER:
    WPA3 offers several key security improvements over WPA2:

    - **Enhanced Encryption:** WPA3 uses SAE (Simultaneous Authentication of Equals)
    instead of WPA2's PSK (Pre-Shared Key) for personal networks, providing stronger
    protection against offline dictionary attacks.
    - **Forward Secrecy:** WPA3 ensures that even if a session key is compromised, past
    communications remain secure, a feature not present in WPA2.
    - **Improved Public Wi-Fi Security:** WPA3 introduces Opportunistic Wireless
    Encryption (OWE), encrypting traffic on open networks without requiring a password.
    - **192-bit Security Mode:** WPA3-Enterprise supports an optional 192-bit encryption
    mode, aligning with CNSA (Commercial National Security Algorithm) Suite requirements
    for high-security environments.
    - **Transition Mode:** WPA3 includes compatibility modes to support mixed WPA2/WPA3
    deployments, easing migration [10].

    These enhancements address vulnerabilities in WPA2, such as susceptibility to KRACK
    attacks, and provide stronger security for modern Wi-Fi networks.

ANSWER:

    signal in the direction of the intended receiver, rather than broadcasting it
    uniformly in all directions. This is achieved through advanced multi-user MIMO (MU-

    create constructive interference at the receiver.

    other devices. This targeted approach is particularly effective in high-density
    environments and for high-bandwidth applications, as it ensures stronger and more
    reliable connections for specific users or devices.

    The use of coordinated beamforming (Co-BF), as outlined in Wi-Fi 8 discussions [8],
    further refines this by enabling access points to synchronize their beamforming
    efforts, minimizing inter-AP interference and optimizing network-wide performance.
